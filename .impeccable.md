# Pasiflow — Design Context

This file is the source of truth for design decisions across Pasiflow. Honor the brand tokens; use taste for everything else.

## Design Context

### Users

**Primary surface in scope:** Detroit Manager Portal (`/[locale]/manager`).

**Primary user:** Justin Carter — Detroit-based deal manager. Daily power-user. Adds 5–20 listings per week from a laptop, sometimes captures notes on his phone while standing in front of a property. He is not a guest browsing the site; he is the worker who lives in this tool every day. His JTBD is: **turn a Detroit property he just walked into a publishable wholesale deal for Turkish investors in under five minutes.**

What he needs from the UI:
- Tabular, scannable numbers (CoC, mortgage, net monthly) — he reads them all day
- Zero ceremony — the wizard should feel like filling out a clipboard, not a marketing site
- Instant feedback on the math — every input should recalculate immediately
- Trust that "Publish" is final — clear separation between draft and live

Secondary user: Erman / admin reviewing Justin's pipeline.

### Brand Personality

Three-word read on the brand: **deliberate, mechanical, opinionated.**

Pasiflow itself sits at the intersection of luxury (gold accent, Detroit "wholesale-to-Turkish-LP" positioning) and operations (the actual product is daily landlord ops). The Manager Portal is the operational face — the back-of-house workshop where the polished investor product gets assembled. So the surface should feel like a **surveyor's notebook**, not a marketing brochure: precise, tabular, confident, with quiet luxury cues coming from typography and the gold accent — never from gradients, glass, or decoration.

Emotional goal: Justin feels **leverage**. He should think "this tool was built for me," not "this is a nice website with a form."

### Aesthetic Direction

- **Theme:** Light. Charcoal `#1F2328` typography on warm off-white `#F5F5F5`. Gold `#C1A05E` is precious — used only for the value line (CoC, totals), the active step, and one publish CTA. Never for decoration.
- **Type direction:** Editorial / pro-tool hybrid. A confident contemporary serif for headings + sectioning, a refined neutral grotesk for UI, and a tabular mono for all financial figures. (Specific picks documented in `app/globals.css`.)
- **Layout direction:** Asymmetric two-column on wide; left column drives inputs, right column is the live "deal sheet" output. Borders are 1px hairlines, never colored stripes. Numbers are right-aligned and tabular.
- **Motion:** Restrained. State transitions and step changes get short ease-out-quart fades (~200ms). No bouncing, no parallax, no celebration confetti.
- **Anti-references:** Linear-style "everything is a card with a gradient." Stripe-clone dashboards with sparklines as decoration. AI-template real-estate sites with hero gradients + glass cards + neon CoC chips. Border-left accent stripes (banned outright).

### Design Principles

1. **The number is the hero.** Every screen has one money figure that matters most (CoC, NET MONTHLY, TOTAL TO WIRE). It gets the gold, the size, the tabular font. Everything else recedes.
2. **Hairlines, not boxes.** Prefer 1px dividers and white-space over filled cards. When a card is needed, it earns its weight with content density, not shadow.
3. **Tabular before everything.** All money, percentages, and counts use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on a mono face. Numbers line up vertically across rows — Justin scans columns, not labels.
4. **Gold is a verb.** Gold appears only on the active step, the final value, and the publish action. If it's on the screen three times, two of them are wrong.
5. **No decoration as personality.** Personality comes from typography, asymmetry, and confident spacing — not from gradients, glass, glow, badges, or sparklines.
